# CODA Strategic Partnership Plan

## Executive proposition

CODA can serve as a China-facing institutional and industrial-development bridge for the Global Halal Digital Trust Ecosystem. The objective is not to centralize sovereign authority, but to create a trusted pathway through which qualified Chinese manufacturers, producers, laboratories, logistics providers, investors and other organizations can participate in an interoperable global ecosystem.

## Strategic pillars

### 1. China market access

Use CODA's overseas-development and international-cooperation networks to identify qualified organizations and projects that may benefit from participation.

### 2. Industrial onboarding

Create a structured pathway for Chinese manufacturers and producers:

AI education → documentation readiness → laboratory evidence → regulatory pathway → digital identity → trusted supply-chain participation → market access.

### 3. Institutional cooperation

Facilitate appropriate introductions among relevant Chinese institutions, Malaysian institutions, industry organizations and international partners. Such introductions do not themselves constitute regulatory approval or certification.

### 4. Trusted logistics

Integrate participating logistics corridors, including Sinotrans where separately agreed, into the evidence and chain-of-custody architecture.

### 5. Scientific evidence

Connect approved laboratory evidence to the digital trust layer while preserving the distinction between laboratory testing and certification authority.

### 6. Investment and commercial development

Develop a pipeline of investable digital-trust, manufacturing, logistics, Halal/Tayyib and ESG opportunities.

### 7. Global expansion

Use China as one strategic gateway into wider international trade corridors rather than treating the partnership as a single-country deployment.

## Operating model

**CODA:** institutional bridge + industrial network + market development + investment facilitation.

**Global Halal:** digital trust infrastructure + evidence + AI + interoperability + ecosystem governance.

**Regulators:** sovereign authority + certification/approval decisions.

**Laboratories:** scientific testing and reports within their lawful scope.

**Manufacturers:** product/process data and compliance participation.

**Logistics:** chain-of-custody execution and event evidence.

**Retailers:** trusted product intake, inventory and consumer access.

**Consumers:** verification and ESG/global-citizen participation.

## Commercial logic

The partnership should be structured around ecosystem growth rather than a simplistic logo-royalty model. Economic value may arise from increased participation, enterprise services, digital trust services, onboarding, market intelligence, logistics integration, ESG services, investment facilitation and other lawful services.

All financial mechanics require negotiation and definitive contractual documentation.

## KPIs

### Institutional

- qualified institutional introductions;
- participating government/industry bodies;
- signed pilot instruments;
- regulatory-workflow readiness.

### Commercial

- manufacturers onboarded;
- products/producers entering the trusted network;
- transaction/service revenue;
- market-access opportunities;
- investment pipeline.

### Technical

- API integrations;
- evidence events recorded;
- verification latency;
- exception rate;
- security incidents;
- system availability.

### Impact

- compliance cycle-time reduction;
- supply-chain visibility;
- counterfeit/fraud risk reduction;
- ESG participation;
- recycling/circular-economy actions;
- consumer verification activity.

## 12-month work programme

**Phase 1 — 0–60 days:** institutional validation, legal due diligence, partner mapping, pilot selection.

**Phase 2 — 60–120 days:** MoU finalization, pilot architecture, API/data mapping, security assessment.

**Phase 3 — 120–240 days:** controlled China–Malaysia pilot, manufacturer onboarding, laboratory/evidence workflows and logistics integration.

**Phase 4 — 240–365 days:** evaluate KPIs, expand participants, prepare additional trade corridors and investment package.

## Non-negotiable principles

1. Sovereignty is preserved.
2. Regulatory authority is not transferred by the platform.
3. Evidence must be verifiable.
4. Sensitive data is disclosed only on an authorized basis.
5. Existing systems can remain in place through APIs.
6. Commercial incentives must be transparent and contractually defined.
7. Government marks and endorsements require explicit authorization.
