# Business Model & Sustainable Economics

## Objective

Build a business that can fund technology, security, governance, research and global expansion without compromising regulatory independence.

## Revenue architecture

### 1. Participation subscriptions
Tiered access for manufacturers, retailers, logistics providers and other commercial participants.

### 2. Digital trust services
Evidence management, traceability, verification workflows, audit preparation, API access and enterprise integrations.

### 3. AI services
Knowledge, process advisory, analytics and commercial services, priced by usage/service bundle.

### 4. Laboratory integration
Digital evidence exchange, chain-of-custody services and enterprise laboratory connectivity where contracted and authorized.

### 5. Retail intelligence
Inventory, demand, provenance and supplier intelligence.

### 6. ESG services
Sustainability evidence, reporting, consumer engagement and circular-economy programmes.

### 7. Market intelligence
Aggregated, governed intelligence products that do not expose restricted personal or confidential commercial data.

### 8. Implementation services
Platform integration, onboarding, training, localization and support.

## Network economics

Value increases as trusted participants join:

`More manufacturers -> more products -> more retailers -> more consumers -> more trusted intelligence -> more value to participants -> more adoption`

## Commercial participation principle

Global Halal should create mutual ecosystem value. A participant should see a commercial reason to join beyond compliance.

For governmental logos, certification marks, licensing, recognition or revenue-sharing arrangements, the platform must use formal agreements and never assume rights.

## Unit economics

The financial model should track:
- customer acquisition cost;
- average revenue per participant;
- gross margin by service;
- infrastructure cost per transaction/evidence event;
- AI inference cost;
- security/compliance cost;
- support cost;
- retention/churn;
- network contribution margin.

## Sustainability rule

Critical trust infrastructure must not depend entirely on grant funding. Commercial services should finance the platform while governance and regulatory functions remain appropriately separated.

## Funding

Possible funding sources include strategic investment, enterprise revenue, government-supported pilot programmes, grants, development finance and Islamic finance structures where legally and commercially appropriate.
