# Governance, Risk & Accountability Framework

## Governance model

### Global layer

Responsible for:
- interoperability standards;
- trust framework;
- common technical policies;
- security baseline;
- evidence interoperability;
- global ecosystem participation rules.

### National layer

Responsible for:
- national law;
- regulator authority;
- data sovereignty;
- approved infrastructure;
- national recognition/authorization;
- local dispute mechanisms.

### Organisational layer

Each manufacturer, laboratory, logistics provider and retailer remains accountable for its own operational controls and submitted information.

## Separation of powers

The platform operator must not unilaterally become the regulator, laboratory, certifier and commercial beneficiary of every workflow. Role separation protects trust.

## Risk register

### Strategic
- geopolitical conflict;
- market adoption failure;
- funding shortfall;
- overdependence on one ecosystem partner.

### Legal/regulatory
- conflicting national requirements;
- data residency restrictions;
- certification-recognition changes;
- misuse of government marks;
- cross-border data transfer restrictions.

### Operational
- incorrect data;
- weak supplier controls;
- laboratory turnaround delays;
- logistics exceptions;
- inaccurate inventory data.

### Cybersecurity
- ransomware;
- insider manipulation;
- credential compromise;
- supply-chain attack;
- API abuse;
- AI prompt/tool abuse.

### Reputational
- false certification claim;
- erroneous consumer information;
- evidence integrity failure;
- unresolved dispute.

## Controls

Each risk must have:
- owner;
- likelihood;
- impact;
- control;
- residual risk;
- trigger;
- response;
- evidence.

## Accountability rule

No AI-generated status is itself a regulator decision. No commercial score can silently alter a legal certification status. Every consequential state change must identify the responsible authority/system and preserve evidence.

## Dispute process

`Complaint -> intake -> evidence preservation -> jurisdiction determination -> investigation -> findings -> correction/decision -> appeal -> closure -> immutable evidence`
