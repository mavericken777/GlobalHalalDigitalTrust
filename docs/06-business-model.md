# Business Model and Economic Architecture

## Objective

The Global Halal entity must be economically sustainable enough to operate critical infrastructure, maintain security, fund research and expand internationally. Revenue should be diversified so no single participant or government becomes a systemic financial dependency.

## Revenue pillars

1. **Participation subscriptions** — tiered access for manufacturers, retailers, laboratories, logistics providers and enterprise partners.
2. **AI services** — onboarding, document intelligence, analytics and enterprise AI capabilities.
3. **Digital trust services** — evidence, credential, verification and audit-support services.
4. **Laboratory integration services** — secure digital workflow and evidence exchange.
5. **ESG services** — verified sustainability workflows and consumer participation infrastructure.
6. **Market intelligence** — permissioned, aggregated analytics; never sell confidential participant data without lawful authorization.
7. **Enterprise/API services** — usage-based access to approved APIs and integrations.
8. **Implementation and assurance services** — integration, training, conformance testing and ecosystem readiness.

Actual fees require market validation and legal/regulatory review.

## Network economics

The economic flywheel is:

`more trusted participants → more useful data/evidence → more retailer and manufacturer value → more consumers and regulators → greater network utility → more participants`

The platform should avoid monetizing regulatory authority itself. Commercial services should be clearly separated from independent certification and governmental decision-making.

## Value by stakeholder

### Manufacturer
Lower administrative friction, reusable evidence, operational intelligence, market insight and better trust with buyers.

### Retailer
Product assurance, inventory intelligence, recall readiness, demand forecasting and supplier visibility.

### Regulator
Higher-quality submissions, evidence continuity, risk analytics and auditability.

### Laboratory
Structured digital workflow, evidence integrity, international interoperability and new commercial reach subject to authorization.

### Consumer
Understandable provenance, trusted status, ESG education and participation.

## Governance of money and data

Revenue governance should include transparent pricing, conflict-of-interest controls, independent audit, separation between regulatory decisions and commercial sales, and strict rules preventing confidential data from becoming an uncontrolled commodity.

## Funding strategy

A phased approach is recommended:

**Phase 1:** founder/institutional funding and strategic grants.

**Phase 2:** paid pilots and enterprise participation.

**Phase 3:** regional network expansion and recurring platform revenue.

**Phase 4:** global ecosystem services and mature intelligence products.

## Commercial principle

The strongest proposition is not charging for a logo. It is creating a trusted network whose participants gain measurable commercial, operational and compliance value by being connected.
