# Governance Framework

## Constitutional principles

### Sovereignty
Every jurisdiction retains authority over its laws, certification decisions and protected information.

### Interoperability
Participants connect through common interfaces without mandatory replacement of national or enterprise systems.

### Evidence
Critical assertions must be supported by verifiable evidence and traceable provenance.

### Human accountability
Consequential regulatory, religious, scientific and commercial decisions have identifiable accountable owners.

### Transparency
Rules, methodologies, conflicts of interest and material incidents should be governed transparently, subject to legitimate confidentiality and security restrictions.

## Proposed governance bodies

1. **Global Governing Council** — strategy, membership and institutional direction.
2. **Sovereignty and Regulatory Council** — jurisdictional participation and regulatory interoperability.
3. **Shariah Governance Council** — Halal/Tayyib principles and religious governance.
4. **Technical Standards Council** — schemas, APIs, interoperability and architecture.
5. **Cybersecurity and Resilience Council** — security assurance and systemic risk.
6. **Scientific/Laboratory Council** — laboratory evidence and scientific interoperability.
7. **Commercial and ESG Council** — market services, economics and sustainability.
8. **Independent Assurance Function** — audits, controls and conflict-of-interest review.

The exact legal structure requires professional legal advice and jurisdiction-specific incorporation analysis.

## Regulator/operator separation

The platform operator should not represent itself as the governmental certification authority unless formally authorized. Commercial services, platform operations and regulatory decisions should have clear organizational separation.

## Participation model

Participants should enter through documented agreements covering identity, permitted data, security controls, evidence responsibilities, liability, incident reporting, intellectual property, termination and dispute resolution.

## Trust registry

A global registry can publish the status of participating organizations, approved credentials, trust policies and interoperability profiles. Registry entries should have provenance and governance history.
